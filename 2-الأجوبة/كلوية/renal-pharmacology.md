### COMPREHENSIVE NEPHROLOGY PHARMACOTHERAPY

## PART I: DIURETICS

### 1.1 Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors

**Drug Name:** Acetazolamide

**Mechanism of Action:** Acetazolamide is a non-competitive, reversible inhibitor of the enzyme carbonic anhydrase (CA), specifically the membrane-bound isoform CA-IV on the luminal brush border and the cytoplasmic isoform CA-II within the proximal tubular epithelial cells. By inhibiting CA, it blocks the rapid dehydration of intraluminal H₂CO₃ to CO₂ + H₂O and the intracellular rehydration of CO₂ + H₂O back to H⁺ and HCO₃⁻. This severely impairs proximal tubular NaHCO₃ reabsorption, leading to delivery of NaHCO₃ to the distal nephron, where it overwhelms reabsorptive capacity. The result is an alkaline diuresis rich in sodium, bicarbonate, and potassium, and the production of a mild hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis.¹

### Indications:

  - *Glaucoma:* Reduction of intraocular pressure by decreasing aqueous humor production by the ciliary body (which depends on CA).
  - *Metabolic Alkalosis:* Correction of metabolic alkalosis, particularly in patients with heart failure receiving high-dose loop diuretics, or in chronic respiratory acidosis with compensatory metabolic alkalosis (post-hypercapnic alkalosis).
  - *Altitude Sickness (Acute Mountain Sickness):* Prophylaxis and treatment by inducing a metabolic acidosis that stimulates ventilation, improving oxygenation during sleep.
  - *Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (Pseudotumor Cerebri).*
  - *Epilepsy (Adjunctive):* In catamenial epilepsy and absence seizures.

### Contraindications:

  - *Sulfonamide Allergy:* Absolute. Acetazolamide is a sulfonamide derivative.
  - *Severe Hepatic Impairment:* The metabolic acidosis can precipitate hepatic encephalopathy by increasing renal vein ammonia delivery.
  - *Severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) with Hypercapnia:* Acidosis can worsen respiratory failure.
  - *Hypokalemia and Hyponatremia:* Relative contraindication; the drug will worsen these.
  - *Adrenocortical Insufficiency (Addison's Disease):* Risk of severe electrolyte derangement.

### Dosage:

  - *Glaucoma:* 250 mg PO every 6-12 hours; IV 250-500 mg.
  - *Edema/Metabolic Alkalosis:* 250-375 mg PO once daily in the morning.
  - *Altitude Sickness:* 125-250 mg PO twice daily, starting 24-48 hours before ascent and continuing for 48 hours at altitude.
  - *Dose Adjustment in Renal Impairment:* Contraindicated if CrCl <10 mL/min. Use with caution in moderate impairment (CrCl 10-50 mL/min) due to risk of acidosis.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Metabolic Acidosis (Hyperchloremic, Normal Anion Gap):* The intended therapeutic effect, but can become severe.
  - *Hypokalemia:* Due to increased distal tubular flow rate and Na⁺ delivery, stimulating K⁺ secretion.
  - *Paresthesias, Tinnitus:* Common, dose-related.
  - *Nephrolithiasis (Calcium Phosphate Stones):* The alkaline urine and reduced urinary citrate promote stone formation.
  - *Hyperammonemia and Hepatic Encephalopathy:* In cirrhotic patients.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Variable; for altitude sickness, short-term prophylaxis. Chronic use requires electrolyte and acid-base monitoring.

### 1.2 Osmotic Diuretics

**Drug Name:** Mannitol

**Mechanism of Action:** Mannitol is a six-carbon, non-absorbable, metabolically inert sugar alcohol. It is freely filtered at the glomerulus and is not reabsorbed by the tubules. Its presence in the tubular fluid creates an osmotic pressure that directly opposes the passive reabsorption of water from the proximal tubule and the thin descending limb of the loop of Henle. This "osmotic obligate" water retention decreases the luminal Na⁺ concentration (diluting the fluid), reducing the gradient for passive Na⁺ reabsorption. The major effect is a massive increase in water excretion, but with a smaller increase in Na⁺ excretion (water diuresis with relatively less natriuresis). The increased tubular flow also reduces the medullary interstitial osmotic gradient ("washout"), impairing the kidney's ability to concentrate urine. Most importantly, by increasing plasma osmolality, mannitol draws water from the intracellular compartment into the extracellular fluid, expanding ECF volume, reducing cell swelling, and reducing blood viscosity.²

### Indications:

  - *Cerebral Edema and Elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP):* The most important indication. The acute hypertonicity created by IV mannitol extracts water from the brain parenchyma, rapidly lowering ICP.
  - *Acute Reduction of Intraocular Pressure:* Before ophthalmological surgery (acute glaucoma).
  - *Forced Diuresis:* To enhance the urinary excretion of certain toxins or drugs (e.g., in rhabdomyolysis to promote a high urine flow rate and flush out myoglobin casts—though aggressive volume expansion with isotonic crystalloids is the mainstay, mannitol has been used).
  - *Prophylaxis of Acute Kidney Injury in Rhabdomyolysis and Transplant Surgery:* Controversial, limited evidence.

### Contraindications:

  - *Established Anuric Acute Kidney Injury:* The drug cannot be filtered or excreted; it remains in the vascular space, causing severe, potentially fatal hypervolemia, pulmonary edema, heart failure, and dilutional hyponatremia.
  - *Severe Dehydration:* Must be corrected first.
  - *Active Intracranial Bleeding:* The temporary decompression of brain tissue could allow further expansion of the hematoma.
  - *Severe Congestive Heart Failure or Pulmonary Edema:* The initial expansion of ECF volume can worsen these.

### Dosage:

  - *Cerebral Edema/ICP:* 0.5-2 g/kg as a 15-25% solution IV infusion over 30-60 minutes. A clinical effect is seen within 15-30 minutes and lasts 4-6 hours. Do not repeat without checking serum osmolality; discontinue if osmolality exceeds 320 mOsm/kg.
  - *Forced Diuresis:* 25-100 g as a 5-25% solution.
  - *Dose in Renal Impairment:* Contraindicated in established oliguric/anuric AKI.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Volume Overload, Pulmonary Edema, Hyponatremia:* From the initial ECF expansion.
  - *Rebound Increase in ICP:* If mannitol crosses a disrupted blood-brain barrier into the cerebral interstitium, it can later draw water into the brain.
  - *Hyperkalemia (due to cellular K⁺ shift with water egress).*
  - *Acute Kidney Injury (Osmotic Nephrosis):* High doses can cause renal tubular cell vacuolization and swelling, leading to AKI.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Acute, short-term administration only, typically for 24-48 hours maximum.

### 1.3 Loop Diuretics

Loop diuretics are the most powerful natriuretic agents available, capable of excreting 20-25% of the filtered load of Na⁺ (compared to ~3-5% for thiazides). They are the drugs of choice when an immediate, large-volume diuretic effect is required.³

**Drug Name:** Furosemide

**Trade Names:** Lasix

**Name Origin:** Derived from fur- (furan ring in its chemical structure) + -semide (common suffix for loop diuretics, originally from sulfonamide).

**Mechanism of Action:** Furosemide is a specific inhibitor of the **Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2)** located on the luminal (apical) membrane of the epithelial cells of the thick ascending limb (TAL) of the loop of Henle. It competes for the Cl⁻ binding site on the transporter. By blocking NKCC2, it abolishes the active reabsorption of NaCl, the single effect that drives the countercurrent multiplier system. This prevents both the generation of a hypertonic medullary interstitium and the dilution of tubular fluid. The massive delivery of Na⁺, Cl⁻, and water to the distal nephron far exceeds the reabsorptive capacity of the DCT and collecting duct, resulting in profound natriuresis and diuresis. The blockade also eliminates the lumen-positive transepithelial potential, causing marked **hypercalciuria** and **hypermagnesuria**. Intravenous furosemide has an immediate, additional pulmonary vasodilatory effect (prostaglandin-mediated) before the onset of diuresis, making it first-line for acute pulmonary edema.⁴

### Contraindications:

  - *Anuria:* Absolute. There must be sufficient GFR to deliver the drug to its site of action.
  - *Severe Hypokalemia or Hyponatremia:* Will be worsened.
  - *Hepatic Coma or Pre-Coma:* Hypokalemia increases renal ammoniagenesis, and hypovolemia can precipitate hepatorenal syndrome.
  - *Sulfonamide Allergy:* Cross-reactivity is possible but rare; consider ethacrynic acid as an alternative.

### Dosage:

  - *Standard Adult Dose:* PO 20-80 mg once or twice daily. Bioavailability of oral furosemide is highly variable, averaging 50% (range 10-100%). Patients with decompensated heart failure often have gut wall edema, which drastically reduces oral absorption, necessitating a switch to IV. The IV:PO conversion is approximately 1:2 (i.e., 40 mg IV = 80 mg PO).
  - *Acute Pulmonary Edema/Hypertensive Emergency:* IV 40-80 mg bolus over 1-2 minutes. If no response, double the dose every 2 hours until effect or maximum dose (up to 500 mg IV total daily dose).
  - *Dose in Advanced CKD (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²):* Tubular secretion of furosemide is impaired, requiring much higher doses (160-500 mg IV) to deliver an effective amount to its tubular site of action.
  - *Diuretic Braking and Resistance:* Continuous IV infusion (e.g., 5-20 mg/hour) after a bolus can overcome post-diuretic sodium retention and "braking" phenomenon.

### Important Side Effects (Mechanism-Based):

  - *Hypokalemic, Hypochloremic Metabolic Alkalosis:* Increased Na⁺ delivery to the principal cells of the collecting duct is reabsorbed via ENaC, generating a large lumen-negative potential that drives K⁺ and H⁺ secretion into the lumen. This is the most predictable complication.
  - *Ototoxicity (Sensorineural Hearing Loss, Tinnitus):* The stria vascularis of the inner ear uses NKCC2 and an isoform (NKCC1) to maintain the high K⁺ concentration in endolymph. Rapid high-dose IV bolus (>4 mg/min) can cause acute, usually reversible ototoxicity. Risk is synergistically increased with concurrent aminoglycosides.
  - *Hyperuricemia:* Acute volume depletion increases proximal tubular uric acid reabsorption, and furosemide competes with urate for the organic anion secretory transporter in the PCT, reducing secretion. This can precipitate acute gout.
  - *Hypomagnesemia and Hypocalciuria:* From inhibition of paracellular transport in the TAL.
  - *Dilutional Hyponatremia:* Rare; more common is true volume depletion with secondary hypertonic hyponatremia (from excess diuretic-induced thirst and water drinking).
  - *NSAID Interaction:* NSAIDs block renal prostaglandin synthesis, reducing renal blood flow and blunting the diuretic effect. This combination can precipitate AKI.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Acute: days for decompensated states. Chronic: lifelong for heart failure with volume overload, with as-needed dose adjustment.

**Drug Name:** Bumetanide

**Trade Names:** Bumex

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as furosemide (NKCC2 inhibitor).

- **Key Distinction from Furosemide:** Bumetanide has significantly higher oral bioavailability (80-100%), meaning the switch from IV to PO does not require as large a dose adjustment. It is roughly 40 times more potent mg-for-mg (1 mg bumetanide ≈ 40 mg furosemide).
- **Dosage:** PO/IV 0.5-2 mg once or twice daily. Maximum 10 mg/day.
- **Ototoxicity Risk:** Potentially higher than furosemide, particularly with rapid IV push.
- **Sulfonamide Cross-Reactivity:** Likely similar to furosemide.

**Drug Name:** Torsemide

**Trade Names:** Demadex

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as furosemide.

- **Key Distinction from Furosemide:** Torsemide has consistently near 100% oral bioavailability, unaffected by food or gut wall edema in heart failure. Its half-life is longer (3-4 hours) than furosemide (1-2 hours), allowing once-daily dosing. Preliminary evidence suggests torsemide may have anti-fibrotic effects (via antagonism of the AT1 receptor or inhibition of aldosterone synthase), potentially leading to better outcomes in heart failure, though major trials (TRANSFORM-HF) showed no mortality difference compared to furosemide.⁵
- **Dosage:** PO/IV 10-200 mg once daily.
- **Sulfonamide Cross-Reactivity:** Likely similar to furosemide.

**Drug Name:** Ethacrynic Acid

**Trade Names:** Edecrin

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as furosemide (NKCC2 inhibitor). It is the only loop diuretic that is **not** a sulfonamide derivative; it is a phenoxyacetic acid derivative.

- **Crucial and Unique Indication:** It is the loop diuretic of choice—and essentially the only loop diuretic that can be safely used—in a patient with a documented, true, severe allergy to sulfonamide-containing drugs, where the benefit of a loop diuretic outweighs the risk.
- **Dosage:** PO 50-200 mg once or twice daily. IV 50-100 mg.
- **Ototoxicity Risk:** The highest of all loop diuretics; must be infused slowly IV.

### 1.4 Thiazide and Thiazide-Like Diuretics

These drugs act on the early distal convoluted tubule (DCT). They inhibit the electroneutral **Na⁺-Cl⁻ cotransporter (NCC)** on the luminal membrane. They block 5-7% of filtered Na⁺ reabsorption. Unlike loop diuretics, they **increase** calcium reabsorption in the DCT and decrease urinary calcium excretion. They are the drug class of choice for idiopathic hypercalciuria and calcium-based nephrolithiasis.⁶

**Drug Name:** Hydrochlorothiazide

**Trade Names:** Microzide

**Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the NCC cotransporter in the DCT. The increased Na⁺ load to the collecting duct stimulates ENaC-mediated Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion, causing hypokalemia. The mechanism of enhanced Ca²⁺ reabsorption involves: (1) volume depletion stimulating proximal Na⁺ and passive Ca²⁺ reabsorption; (2) inhibition of NCC lowers intracellular Na⁺ in DCT cells, enhancing the driving force for the basolateral Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX1).⁶

### Contraindications:

  - *Anuria.*
  - *Sulfonamide Allergy:* Hydrochlorothiazide is a sulfonamide.
  - *Gout:* The hyperuricemic effect can precipitate acute attacks.

### Dosage:

  - *Hypertension:* 12.5-50 mg PO once daily. Evidence favors lower doses (12.5-25 mg) for BP control, minimizing metabolic side effects.
  - *Edema:* 25-100 mg PO once daily.
  - *Nephrolithiasis:* 25 mg or 50 mg once or twice daily.
  - *Dose in Renal Impairment:* Ineffective and not recommended when eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m².

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Hypokalemia, Hypomagnesemia, Metabolic Alkalosis.*
  - *Hypercalcemia:* The hallmark metabolic effect, distinguishing them from loop diuretics.
  - *Hyperuricemia and Acute Gout.*
  - *Hyperglycemia and Impaired Glucose Tolerance:* Attributed to reduced insulin secretion, secondary to hypokalemia, and potentially direct effects on pancreatic beta-cells.
  - *Hyponatremia:* Particularly dangerous and common in elderly women; thiazides impair free water clearance to a greater extent than loop diuretics.
  - *Dyslipidemia:* Mild increase in total cholesterol and triglycerides.
- **Duration:** Chronic, lifelong for hypertension.

**Drug Name:** Chlorthalidone

**Trade Names:** Thalitone

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as hydrochlorothiazide (NCC inhibitor), but with a key difference: its half-life is significantly longer (40-60 hours) compared to HCTZ (6-9 hours). This provides more sustained blood pressure control over 24 hours.

- **Clinical Superiority in Hypertension:** Based on the ALLHAT trial and subsequent meta-analyses, chlorthalidone is now considered the preferred thiazide-type diuretic for the treatment of hypertension due to its proven superiority in reducing cardiovascular events, a benefit not clearly demonstrated to the same extent with equipotent doses of hydrochlorothiazide.⁷
- **Dosage:** Hypertension: 12.5-25 mg PO once daily. Edema: 50-100 mg PO once daily.
- **Side Effects:** Same profile as HCTZ, but the risk of hyponatremia and hypokalemia is more pronounced at higher doses due to its prolonged action.

**Drug Name:** Metolazone

**Trade Names:** Zaroxolyn

**Mechanism of Action:** Thiazide-like diuretic; inhibits NCC. A unique feature is that it also has some effect in the proximal tubule (inhibiting carbonic anhydrase to a small degree). Metolazone retains some diuretic efficacy even in advanced renal impairment (eGFR <20 mL/min/1.73 m²), where thiazides like HCTZ become largely ineffective. This makes it the thiazide of choice for patients with resistant edema due to severe CKD or refractory heart failure.⁸

- **Crucial Indication: Sequential Nephron Blockade:** Metolazone is classically used in combination with a loop diuretic (e.g., furosemide). The loop diuretic blocks the TAL, but compensatory Na⁺ reabsorption increases in the DCT. Adding metolazone 15-30 minutes before the loop diuretic blocks this distal compensation, creating a profound synergistic diuresis. This is reserved for severe, diuretic-resistant edema, and mandates rigorous, often daily, monitoring of potassium, magnesium, and volume status, as the risk of fatal electrolyte disturbances and AKI is high.
- **Dosage:** 2.5-5 mg PO once daily. For synergy, typically 5-10 mg 30 minutes before loop diuretic, titrating down to the minimal effective dose.

### 1.5 Potassium-Sparing Diuretics: Epithelial Sodium Channel Blockers

**Drug Name:** Amiloride

**Trade Names:** Midamor

**Mechanism of Action:** Amiloride directly and reversibly blocks the **epithelial sodium channel (ENaC)** on the apical membrane of the principal cells of the collecting duct. By reducing Na⁺ entry, it reduces the lumen-negative transepithelial potential, which is the driving force for K⁺ and H⁺ secretion. The result is a mild natriuresis with a simultaneous retention of potassium and acid. Its action is independent of aldosterone.⁹

### Contraindications:

  - *Hyperkalemia (K⁺ >5.5 mEq/L).*
  - *Renal Impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²):* High risk of hyperkalemia.
  - *Concurrent Potassium Supplementation or Other K⁺-Sparing Agents.*
- **Dosage:** 5-10 mg PO once daily (maximum 20 mg/day).

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Life-Threatening Hyperkalemia:* The most critical risk.
  - *Metabolic Acidosis (Hyperchloremic, Normal Anion Gap):* Analogous to a Type IV renal tubular acidosis, because inhibition of ENaC reduces H⁺ secretion.

### Unique Uses:

  - *Liddle's Syndrome (Pseudohyperaldosteronism):* This is a gain-of-function mutation of the ENaC beta or gamma subunit, causing constitutive channel activity. Amiloride directly inhibits this overactive channel and is the specific, targeted therapy (as opposed to spironolactone, which would be ineffective since the disease is not aldosterone-driven).
  - *Lithium-Induced Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus:* Amiloride blocks the uptake of lithium by ENaC into principal cells, thereby reducing the concentration of intracellular lithium that interferes with the ADH-cAMP-AQP2 pathway.

**Drug Name:** Triamterene

**Trade Names:** Dyrenium

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as amiloride (ENaC blocker).

- **Key Difference:** Triamterene carries a risk of causing crystalluria and acute kidney injury due to the formation of triamterene stones or crystal nephropathy. It is also a known cause of pseudo-nephrolithiasis (its crystals are radiolucent, mimicking uric acid stones). Amiloride does not share this risk. It is rarely used as a first-line agent.

### 1.6 Potassium-Sparing Diuretics: Aldosterone Receptor Antagonists (MRAs)

**Drug Name:** Spironolactone

**Trade Names:** Aldactone

**Mechanism of Action:** Spironolactone is a synthetic steroid that acts as a competitive, pharmacologic antagonist of the **mineralocorticoid receptor (MR)** in the principal cells of the collecting duct. It binds to the MR, preventing the binding of aldosterone. This prevents aldosterone-induced translocation of the receptor-hormone complex to the nucleus and the subsequent transcription of ENaC, Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase, and ROMK channels. The net effect is a reduction in Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ and H⁺ secretion. Its action is dependent on the presence of endogenous or exogenous aldosterone. It is most effective when aldosterone levels are high (e.g., cirrhosis with ascites, heart failure, nephrotic syndrome). It is also a non-selective antagonist of the androgen receptor, leading to its key anti-androgen side effects.¹⁰

### Contraindications:

  - *Hyperkalemia (K⁺ >5.5 mEq/L).*
  - *Severe Renal Impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²).*
  - *Addison's Disease (Primary Adrenal Insufficiency):* Patients cannot produce aldosterone and rely on salt retention; MR blockade will cause fatal hyperkalemia.
  - *Pregnancy and Lactation:* Feminization of the male fetus.

### Dosage:

  - *Heart Failure (RALES Trial):* Starting dose 12.5-25 mg PO once daily, titrated to 25-50 mg once daily. This is a proven mortality-reducing therapy in HFrEF (NYHA class III-IV).
  - *Resistant Hypertension:* 25-50 mg PO once daily.
  - *Ascites in Cirrhosis:* 100-400 mg/day.

### Important Side Effects (Endocrine/Sexual):

  - *Gynecomastia (painful, often unilateral), Breast Tenderness, Impotence, Decreased Libido in Men, Menstrual Irregularities in Women:* These are due to the anti-androgenic actions (blockade of the testosterone receptor and inhibition of 17,20-desmolase, reducing testosterone synthesis). These effects are dose-related and often lead to drug discontinuation.
  - *Life-Threatening Hyperkalemia.*
  - *Metabolic Acidosis.*

**Drug Name:** Eplerenone

**Trade Names:** Inspra

**Mechanism of Action:** Selective MR antagonist, structurally distinct from spironolactone. It has a 100- to 1000-fold lower affinity for androgen and progesterone receptors, resulting in a far better endocrine side-effect profile (significantly less gynecomastia). It is less potent mg-for-mg than spironolactone.¹¹

- **Major Trial:** EPHESUS trial showed a mortality benefit in patients with left ventricular dysfunction after acute myocardial infarction.
- **Dosage:** Heart Failure: Start 25 mg PO once daily, titrate to 50 mg once daily. Hypertension: 50-100 mg/day.
- **Contraindications:** Same as spironolactone. It is a major substrate of CYP3A4; concurrent use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin) is contraindicated due to risk of hyperkalemia.

### 1.7 Vasopressin Receptor Antagonists (Vaptans)

**Drug Name:** Tolvaptan

**Trade Names:** Samsca, Jinarc

**Mechanism of Action:** Tolvaptan is a highly selective, competitive antagonist of the **V2 vasopressin receptor** on the basolateral membrane of principal cells of the collecting duct. By blocking ADH binding, it prevents the activation of adenylyl cyclase and the insertion of aquaporin-2 (AQP2) water channels into the luminal membrane. This renders the collecting duct impermeable to water, even in the presence of high circulating ADH levels, resulting in a pure **aquaresis**—the excretion of a large volume of electrolyte-free water (solute-poor urine). Unlike diuretics, it does not cause significant natriuresis or kaluresis.¹²

### Contraindications:

  - *Hypovolemic Hyponatremia:* Will cause fatal volume depletion.
  - *Anuria:* The patient must be able to produce urine.
  - *Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir):* Will massively increase tolvaptan exposure, causing unpredictable, severe aquaresis.
  - *Severe Hepatic Impairment or History of Liver Injury:* Black Box Warning (see below).
  - *Polycystic Kidney Disease patients with a need for urgent urinary concentration:* Not applicable.

### Dosage:

  - *Euvolemic/Hypervolemic Hyponatremia:* 15 mg PO once daily, titrated to 30-60 mg PO once daily based on serum Na⁺. Must be initiated in a hospital setting to allow for close monitoring of serum sodium (avoiding overly rapid correction, which risks osmotic demyelination syndrome).
  - *Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD) (Jinarc brand):* Approved to slow the decline in kidney function and total kidney volume growth in adults with rapidly progressive, high-risk ADPKD (typically CKD stage G1-G3a). The regimen involves a split dose: 45 mg on waking + 15 mg 8 hours later, titrated to 90 mg + 30 mg, based on tolerability (aquaresis). This requires a profound commitment to high water intake (up to 10-12 L/day) to prevent thirst and dehydration.
- **Black Box Warning (Hepatotoxicity):** Tolvaptan can cause serious, potentially irreversible, and fatal liver injury. Liver enzymes (ALT, AST, bilirubin) must be monitored strictly: monthly for the first 18 months, then every 3 months. The drug is immediately discontinued if ALT rises >3 times the upper limit of normal, and permanently discontinued if signs of hepatic injury occur.

**Drug Name:** Conivaptan

**Mechanism of Action:** A non-selective V1a/V2 vasopressin receptor antagonist. It is only available as an intravenous formulation. The V1a blockade causes splanchnic vasodilation, which could theoretically worsen portal hypertension in cirrhotic patients.

- **Contraindications:** Hypovolemic hyponatremia, concurrent CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- **Dosage:** IV 20 mg loading dose over 30 minutes, followed by a continuous infusion of 20-40 mg/day for 2-4 days. Use is limited to hospitalized patients with euvolemic hyponatremia.

## PART II: INHIBITORS OF THE RENIN-ANGIOTENSIN-ALDOSTERONE SYSTEM (RAAS)

RAAS inhibitors are the cornerstone of renoprotective therapy, reducing intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria.

### 2.1 Direct Renin Inhibitors

**Drug Name:** Aliskiren

**Trade Names:** Tekturna

**Mechanism of Action:** Aliskiren is a non-peptide, direct, competitive inhibitor of renin. It binds to the active catalytic site of the renin molecule, preventing its cleavage of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I. This blocks the RAAS cascade at its rate-limiting, initial enzymatic step. This leads to a profound reduction in plasma renin activity (PRA), angiotensin I, and angiotensin II.¹³

### Contraindications:

  - *Pregnancy (FDA Category D):* Direct fetal toxicity, neonatal death.
  - *Diabetes Mellitus with Concomitant ACE inhibitor or ARB:* The ALTITUDE trial demonstrated an increased risk of adverse cardiovascular and renal outcomes (hyperkalemia, hypotension, AKI) when aliskiren was added to ACEi/ARB in diabetic patients with CKD. This combination is now contraindicated.¹⁴
  - *Concurrent strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (cyclosporine, itraconazole).*
- **Dosage:** 150-300 mg PO once daily. Onset is 2 weeks; it has a very long plasma half-life (40 hours).

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Diarrhea (severe, leading to discontinuation):* A prominent, dose-related side effect in a significant minority of patients, unique among RAAS blockers.
  - *Hyperkalemia, Hypotension, Reduction in GFR.*
  - *Angioedema:* Lower risk than ACE inhibitors, but still present.
- **Clinical Role:** Due to the ALTITUDE trial results and lack of outcome superiority over ACEi/ARBs, aliskiren is now rarely prescribed. Its primary niche is for patients with a genuine, documented contraindication or intolerance to both ACE inhibitors and ARBs.

### 2.2 Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors

This class is a first-line therapy for hypertension, diabetic and non-diabetic chronic kidney disease with albuminuria, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, and post-MI systolic dysfunction. They reduce proteinuria and slow the progression of CKD independent of their blood pressure-lowering effect, primarily by reducing angiotensin II-mediated efferent arteriolar constriction.¹⁵

**Drug Name:** Lisinopril

**Trade Names:** Prinivil, Zestril

**Mechanism of Action:** Lisinopril is the lysine analog of enalaprilat. It competitively inhibits angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), a dipeptidyl carboxypeptidase. This prevents the conversion of angiotensin I to the potent vasoconstrictor angiotensin II, and also prevents the degradation of bradykinin (a potent vasodilator). The renal effect is a reduction in efferent arteriolar resistance, which decreases intraglomerular hydrostatic pressure (P_GC) and lowers the filtration fraction. This hemodynamic effect reduces proteinuria and is renoprotective in the long term, though it causes an acute, predictable, and reversible decline in GFR.¹⁵

### Contraindications:

  - *Pregnancy (Absolute):* Fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, anuria, neonatal death.
  - *History of ACEi-Induced Angioedema:* Absolute.
  - *Bilateral Renal Artery Stenosis (or Stenosis in a Solitary Kidney):* The efferent vasodilation in a kidney already underperfused due to a proximal stenosis can cause catastrophic loss of GFR and AKI.

### Dosage:

  - *Hypertension/CKD Proteinuria:* Start 5-10 mg PO once daily, titrate to 20-40 mg once daily.
  - *Heart Failure:* Start 2.5-5 mg PO once daily, titrate carefully as tolerated.
  - *Dose in Renal Impairment:* Require dose reduction as GFR declines. Lisinopril is not a prodrug, is 100% renally excreted, and is removed by hemodialysis. Dose after dialysis.

### Predictable and Important Side Effects:

  - *Acute Kidney Injury (Hemodynamic):* An expected drop in eGFR of 15-30% within the first 2-4 weeks is acceptable and does not mandate drug discontinuation. A >30% drop or a continued decline warrants investigation for occult renal artery stenosis, volume depletion, or NSAID use.
  - *Dry, Non-Productive Cough:* Occurs in 5-20% of patients, attributed to the accumulation of bradykinin and substance P in the lungs, which are normally degraded by ACE. It is not dose-related and requires switching to an ARB.
  - *Angioedema (Life-Threatening):* Swelling of the lips, tongue, face, and larynx. Incidence ~0.3-0.7%, but higher in black populations. It can occur at any time during treatment, not just at initiation. Requires immediate airway management and permanent avoidance of ACEi and ARBs.
  - *Hyperkalemia.*
  - *Dysgeusia (Loss of Taste), Skin Rash.*

**Drug Name:** Ramipril

**Trade Names:** Altace

**Mechanism of Action:** Same as lisinopril. Ramipril is a prodrug, converted in the liver to the active metabolite ramiprilat. It has high tissue ACE-binding affinity.

- **Major Trial:** The HOPE (Heart Outcomes Prevention Evaluation) trial established the vascular protective and renal outcome benefits of ramipril in high-risk patients without left ventricular dysfunction.
- **Dosage:** 2.5-10 mg PO once daily (may be divided twice daily). Dose reduction in renal impairment.

**Drug Name:** Enalapril

**Trade Names:** Vasotec

**Mechanism of Action:** Prodrug, metabolized in the liver to the active enalaprilat. The only ACE inhibitor available in IV form (enalaprilat).

- **Dosage:** PO 2.5-40 mg/day in 1-2 divided doses. IV enalaprilat 1.25-5 mg every 6 hours.

**Drug Name:** Captopril

**Trade Names:** Capoten

**Mechanism of Action:** The original ACE inhibitor; not a prodrug. Contains a sulfhydryl group, which is associated with a higher risk of skin rash, dysgeusia, and proteinuria (membranous nephropathy at very high doses used historically).

- **Dosage:** Short half-life. 25-50 mg PO three times daily.

**Drug Name:** Benazepril, Fosinopril

- *Fosinopril* has balanced hepatic and renal elimination and requires no dose adjustment in renal impairment.

### 2.3 Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor Blockers (ARBs)

ARBs share all the hemodynamic and renoprotective benefits of ACE inhibitors. The key difference is their side-effect profile: they do not increase bradykinin levels, so **cough and angioedema are significantly less frequent** (though angioedema can still occur, rarely).¹⁶

**Drug Name:** Losartan

**Trade Names:** Cozaar

**Mechanism of Action:** Selective, competitive antagonist of the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor. Blockade of the AT1 receptor prevents all the known deleterious effects of angiotensin II (vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, proximal Na⁺ reabsorption, efferent arteriolar constriction, myocardial fibrosis). The unopposed stimulation of the AT2 receptor by the displaced angiotensin II may theoretically lead to beneficial vasodilation and anti-fibrotic effects.¹⁶

- **Unique Property:** Losartan is the only ARB with a uricosuric effect. It inhibits the urate transporter URAT1 in the proximal tubule, leading to a modest, persistent reduction in serum uric acid (by 0.5-1.0 mg/dL). This makes it a particularly attractive choice in a hypertensive patient with gout or hyperuricemia.
- **Major Trial:** RENAAL trial demonstrated a significant reduction in the progression of proteinuric diabetic nephropathy (type 2 diabetes) to end-stage renal disease.
- **Dosage:** 50-100 mg PO once daily. Dose adjustment not needed for renal impairment.

**Drug Name:** Valsartan

**Trade Names:** Diovan

**Dosage:** 80-320 mg PO once daily. Also available in combination with sacubitril (ARNI).

**Drug Name:** Irbesartan

**Trade Names:** Avapro

**Dosage:** 150-300 mg PO once daily. The IDNT and IRMA-2 trials established its renoprotective effects.

**Drug Name:** Telmisartan

**Trade Names:** Micardis

**Unique Property:** Telmisartan is a partial agonist of the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma (PPAR-γ), acting like a mild insulin sensitizer. It has the longest half-life of all ARBs (24 hours).

- **Dosage:** 40-80 mg PO once daily.

### 2.4 Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI)

**Drug Name:** Sacubitril/Valsartan

**Trade Names:** Entresto

**Mechanism of Action:** This is a first-in-class, fixed-dose combination containing sacubitril (a neprilysin inhibitor prodrug) and valsartan (an ARB). Sacubitril is metabolized to sacubitrilat, which inhibits neprilysin. Neprilysin is the neutral endopeptidase that degrades several vasoactive peptides, including the natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP, CNP), bradykinin, and adrenomedullin. By inhibiting neprilysin, sacubitril increases the bioavailability of these endogenous peptides, leading to enhanced natriuresis, diuresis, vasodilation, and inhibition of fibrosis and hypertrophy. This effect is powerfully synergistic with the simultaneous ARB blockade of the AT1 receptor, which prevents the reflex activation of the RAAS that would otherwise counteract the effects of the natriuretic peptides and also prevents neprilysin-mediated angiotensin II build-up.¹⁷

### Contraindications:

  - *Pregnancy:* Same as ACEi/ARB.
  - *History of Angioedema with ACEi/ARB.*
  - *Concomitant ACE Inhibitor:* The combination significantly increases the risk of angioedema and is absolutely contraindicated. An ACE inhibitor must be discontinued for a **36-hour washout period** before initiating sacubitril/valsartan.
  - *Severe Hepatic Impairment (Child-Pugh C).*
- **Major Trial:** The PARADIGM-HF trial showed a highly significant 20% reduction in cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization with sacubitril/valsartan compared to enalapril in patients with chronic HFrEF. Subsequent trials (PARAGON-HF) have shown benefit in HFpEF, particularly in patients with LVEF below normal.

### Dosage:

  - *Heart Failure:* Start 24/26 mg or 49/51 mg PO twice daily, titrated to target dose of 97/103 mg PO twice daily.
  - *Dose in Renal Impairment:* Start low dose if eGFR <30. Not recommended if eGFR <15.
  - *Dose in Hepatic Impairment:* Start low if Child-Pugh B.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Hypotension (Symptomatic):* More common than with ACEi alone, due to enhanced vasodilation.
  - *Hyperkalemia.*
  - *Angioedema.*
  - *Increase in BNP:* This is a predictable, pharmacodynamic effect. Since BNP is a substrate for neprilysin, its levels rise. Therefore, BNP is no longer a reliable marker of heart failure decompensation in a patient on sacubitril/valsartan. In contrast, NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate, so its level remains a valid biomarker.

### 2.5 Aldosterone Synthase Inhibitors

**Drug Name:** Baxdrostat (CIN-107)

**Mechanism of Action:** A highly selective, potent inhibitor of the CYP11B2 enzyme (aldosterone synthase). Unlike MRAs (spironolactone, eplerenone) that block the receptor, baxdrostat directly inhibits the terminal enzymatic step of aldosterone biosynthesis in the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal gland. This lowers circulating aldosterone levels, reducing mineralocorticoid receptor activation. Because it is selective for CYP11B2 and spares CYP11B1 (11-beta-hydroxylase, required for cortisol synthesis) at the doses used, it does not suppress glucocorticoid production.¹⁸

- **Status:** Investigational. Phase 2/3 clinical trials (BrigHTN, HALO) have demonstrated significant, sustained reductions in blood pressure in patients with treatment-resistant hypertension. Not yet FDA-approved. Represents a novel mechanism for controlling volume and potassium homeostasis without the endocrine side effects of spironolactone.

## PART III: SODIUM-GLUCOSE COTRANSPORTER 2 (SGLT2) INHIBITORS

SGLT2 inhibitors have emerged as a revolutionary drug class in nephrology, moving from glucose-lowering agents to foundational, organ-protective therapies for both diabetic and non-diabetic chronic kidney disease and heart failure, irrespective of baseline glycemic status.¹⁹

**Drug Names:** Dapagliflozin (Farxiga), Empagliflozin (Jardiance), Canagliflozin (Invokana), Ertugliflozin (Steglatro)

**Mechanism of Action:** They are potent, selective, competitive inhibitors of the SGLT2 protein, the high-capacity, low-affinity Na⁺-glucose cotransporter located on the S1/S2 segment of the proximal convoluted tubule. Under normal conditions, SGLT2 reabsorbs ~90% of the filtered glucose load. Its inhibition induces a therapeutic glucosuria of ~60-100 grams of glucose per day, lowering plasma glucose independently of insulin. More importantly for organ protection, SGLT2 inhibitors block the coupled reabsorption of Na⁺ with glucose in the proximal tubule, leading to increased delivery of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ to the macula densa. This is sensed by the JGA, which, through the tubuloglomerular feedback mechanism, signals **afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction**. This reduces intraglomerular pressure, GFR, and hyperfiltration. The resultant reduction in the filtration fraction and the workload of the proximal tubule is believed to be the primary mechanism for the profound, long-term slowing of CKD progression.²⁰

### Renal Indications (beyond Type 2 Diabetes):

  - *Chronic Kidney Disease with Albuminuria (UACR >200 mg/g):* The DAPA-CKD trial demonstrated that dapagliflozin significantly reduced the risk of a composite outcome of sustained eGFR decline ≥50%, end-stage kidney disease, or death from renal causes in patients with CKD (eGFR 25-75), with and without type 2 diabetes. This led to regulatory approval for CKD irrespective of diabetes.²¹
  - *Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF):* Mortality and hospitalization benefit, independent of diabetes.
  - *Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF):* Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin have shown benefit in reducing heart failure hospitalizations.

### Contraindications:

  - *Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus:* Significant risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).
  - *End-Stage Kidney Disease on Dialysis:* No efficacy data.
  - *Pregnancy, Lactation.*

### Dosage:

  - *Empagliflozin:* 10 mg PO once daily for renoprotection; can increase to 25 mg for glucose lowering.
  - *Dapagliflozin:* 10 mg PO once daily.
  - *Canagliflozin:* 100 mg PO once daily for renoprotection.
  - *Dose in Renal Impairment for Renoprotection:* The renal protective effect is sustained even as eGFR falls. Initiation is approved down to an eGFR of 20-25 mL/min/1.73 m² for dapagliflozin and empagliflozin. The glucose-lowering effect wanes as eGFR falls below 45, but the hemodynamic and organ-protective effects persist.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):* A rare but life-threatening adverse event. It can occur with normal or mildly elevated blood glucose levels (<250 mg/dL). The mechanism involves increased ketone body reabsorption, enhanced glucagon secretion, and reduced insulin secretion. Precipitating factors include surgery, acute illness, reduced caloric intake, and alcohol use. The "sick day" protocol mandates withholding the drug and checking blood or urine ketones.
  - *Genital Mycotic Infections (Balantitis, Vulvovaginitis):* Very common (5-10% incidence) due to the persistent glucosuria promoting fungal growth.
  - *Volume Depletion, Orthostatic Hypotension:* Due to the osmotic diuresis and natriuresis, especially in the elderly and those on loop diuretics.
  - *Fournier's Gangrene (Necrotizing Fasciitis of the Perineum):* An extremely rare but devastating adverse event reported with this class.
  - *Acute Kidney Injury (Initial eGFR Dip):* Similar to RAAS inhibitors, an initial, reversible hemodynamic dip in eGFR of 5-10% is expected and is a marker of effective reduction in glomerular hyperfiltration.

## PART IV: GLUCAGON-LIKE PEPTIDE-1 (GLP-1) RECEPTOR AGONISTS

**Drug Names:** Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), Liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda), Dulaglutide (Trulicity)

**Mechanism of Action:** These are analogs of the incretin hormone GLP-1. They bind to and activate the GLP-1 receptor, which has multiple systemic effects. In the context of renoprotection, the FLOW trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduces the risk of clinically important kidney outcomes and cardiovascular death in people with type 2 diabetes and CKD. The mechanisms are likely multifaceted: reduction in glucose and blood pressure, weight loss, direct anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects on the kidney, and reduction in albuminuria.²²

- **Contraindications:** Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Gastrointestinal (Nausea, Vomiting, Diarrhea):* Very common, usually transient upon dose escalation.
  - *Acute Pancreatitis, Cholecystitis.*
- **Dosage:** Subcutaneous injection, starting low and titrating up to therapeutic dose (e.g., semaglutide 1.0 mg SC once weekly).

## PART V: NON-STEROIDAL MINERALOCORTICOID RECEPTOR ANTAGONISTS

**Drug Name:** Finerenone (Kerendia)

**Mechanism of Action:** Finerenone is a novel, non-steroidal, selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA). Unlike the steroidal MRAs (spironolactone, eplerenone), finerenone has a different molecular structure, tissue distribution (balanced cardiac and renal tissue distribution), and a different cofactor recruitment profile. It potently blocks MR-mediated inflammation, fibrosis, and hypertrophy in the kidney and heart. Crucially, it has a lower incidence of hyperkalemia compared to steroidal MRAs at equi-effective doses, and it does not have anti-androgenic side effects.²³

- **Indications:** To reduce the risk of sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI, and hospitalization for heart failure in adults with **CKD associated with Type 2 Diabetes**. The FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD pivotal trials established its efficacy.

### Contraindications:

  - *Hyperkalemia >5.0 mEq/L at baseline.*
  - *Severe Hepatic Impairment (Child-Pugh C).*
  - *Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (itraconazole, ritonavir).*

### Dosage:

  - Start 10 mg PO once daily if eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m². Start 20 mg if eGFR 25-59. Titrate to 20 mg after 4 weeks if serum K⁺ ≤4.8 mEq/L.
  - Do not initiate if eGFR <25 mL/min/1.73 m². Continue if already on it until eGFR <15 or dialysis initiation.
- **Monitoring:** Serum potassium must be checked 4 weeks after initiation, 4 weeks after a dose increase, and regularly thereafter. Finerenone is a major addition to the CKD toolkit, addressing the residual risk of CKD progression that persists despite ACEi/ARB and SGLT2 inhibitor therapy.

## PART VI: DRUGS FOR HYPERKALEMIA (EMERGENCY AND CHRONIC)

The treatment of life-threatening hyperkalemia follows a specific, sequential order based on mechanism and speed of action.

### 6.1 Stabilize the Cardiac Membrane (Step 1)

**Drug Name:** Calcium Gluconate or Calcium Chloride

**Mechanism of Action:** Calcium ions do not lower serum potassium. They immediately and directly antagonize the electrophysiological effects of hyperkalemia on the cardiac myocyte membrane. Hyperkalemia depolarizes the resting membrane potential, bringing it closer to the threshold potential, leading to a rapid upstroke of phase 0 of the action potential, causing widened QRS and eventually ventricular fibrillation. High extracellular calcium restores the electrical gradient across the sodium channels by shifting the threshold potential to a more positive value, stabilizing the membrane and preventing re-entrant arrhythmias. This effect is transient and lasts 20-60 minutes.²⁴

- **Indication:** Any patient with hyperkalemia and ECG changes (peaked T waves, loss of P waves, widened QRS, sine wave pattern). NOT indicated for asymptomatic hyperkalemia without ECG changes.
- **Dosage:** 1 gram of calcium chloride (13.6 mEq Ca²⁺ in 10 mL) or 2-3 grams of calcium gluconate (9.4-14.1 mEq Ca²⁺ in 10 mL) infused intravenously over 2-3 minutes. Onset: 1-3 minutes. Can repeat every 10 minutes if ECG changes persist.
- **Caution:** In digoxin toxicity, calcium can theoretically worsen "stone heart" phenomenon, though evidence is weak. If digoxin toxicity is suspected, the dose must be diluted in 100 mL of D5W and infused over 30 minutes.

### 6.2 Shift Potassium Into Cells (Step 2, Rapid but Temporary)

**Drug Name:** Insulin and Dextrose

**Mechanism of Action:** Insulin, via binding to the insulin receptor, directly and rapidly stimulates the activity and translocation of the Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase pump to the cell membrane of skeletal muscle and liver. This drives K⁺ from the extracellular fluid into the intracellular compartment. Dextrose is administered concurrently to prevent iatrogenic hypoglycemia.²⁵

- **Dosage:** 10 units of regular insulin IV push, accompanied by 1 ampule (50 mL) of 50% dextrose (D50W) IV, unless the patient is already severely hyperglycemic (>250 mg/dL), in which case dextrose may be omitted. Onset: 10-20 minutes. Duration: 2-4 hours. Monitor blood glucose hourly for several hours to detect rebound hypoglycemia.

**Drug Name:** Beta-2 Adrenergic Agonist (Albuterol)

**Mechanism of Action:** Beta-2 receptor stimulation activates adenylyl cyclase and increases intracellular cAMP, which stimulates the Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase, driving K⁺ into cells.

- **Dosage:** 10-20 mg nebulized albuterol in 4 mL of normal saline, inhaled over 10 minutes. This is 4-8 times the standard bronchodilator dose. Onset: 30 minutes. Duration: 2-4 hours. Note: 20-40% of patients with ESRD are resistant to the K⁺-lowering effect of albuterol.

**Drug Name:** Sodium Bicarbonate

**Mechanism of Action:** The rise in blood pH from a NaHCO₃ infusion shifts H⁺ out of cells. To maintain electroneutrality, K⁺ moves into cells in exchange for H⁺.

- **Dosage:** 1-2 ampules (50-100 mEq) of hypertonic (8.4%) NaHCO₃ IV over 5-10 minutes. **Evidence Note:** Its efficacy as a sole agent for acute hyperkalemia is inconsistent and often disappointing. It is most effective in patients with pre-existing metabolic acidosis. It is a hypertonic solution and carries a risk of volume overload and hypernatremia in ESRD patients.

### 6.3 Remove Potassium From the Body (Step 3, Slow and Definitive)

**Drug Name:** Sodium Zirconium Cyclosilicate (Lokelma)

**Mechanism of Action:** A non-absorbed, inorganic, microporous crystalline compound. It is a highly selective cation exchanger that preferentially exchanges hydrogen and sodium for potassium and ammonium ions throughout the entire gastrointestinal tract. It binds K⁺ in the gut lumen in exchange for Na⁺, trapping it and preventing its absorption. The captured K⁺ is then excreted in the feces.²⁶

- **Dosage:** For acute hyperkalemia correction: 10 g PO three times daily for up to 48 hours. For chronic maintenance: 10 g PO once daily, titrated down to 5 g PO every other day. Onset: 1 hour. Duration of effect: 4 hours.
- **Advantage:** Highly specific for K⁺, does not cause hypomagnesemia or fluid retention.
- **Side Effect:** Edema, due to sodium load (each 5 g dose contains ~400 mg of sodium).

**Drug Name:** Patiromer (Veltassa)

**Mechanism of Action:** A non-absorbed, organic polymer (calcium patiromer sorbitex). It exchanges calcium for potassium in the distal colon, where K⁺ concentration is highest. The potassium-bound polymer is then excreted in the feces.²⁷

- **Dosage:** Start 8.4 g PO once daily, titrated to 25.2 g once daily. Onset: 4-7 hours.
- **Clinically Critical Drug Interaction:** Patiromer binds many oral drugs in the GI tract if taken concurrently. The dosing separation is strict: all other oral medications must be taken at least 3 hours before or 3 hours after patiromer.
- **Side Effect:** Hypomagnesemia (exchanges calcium for magnesium as well).

**Drug Name:** Loop Diuretics (Furosemide)

- **Mechanism:** Increase distal tubular flow and Na⁺ delivery, maximizing kaliuresis. Only effective if the patient is not anuric and has residual renal function.

## PART VII: DRUGS FOR HYPONATREMIA

### 7.1 Vaptans (Tolvaptan, Conivaptan)

See Section 1.7. Indicated for clinically significant, euvolemic (SIADH) or hypervolemic (heart failure, cirrhosis) hyponatremia.

### 7.2 Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl)

- **Indication:** Acute, severe, symptomatic hyponatremia (Na⁺ <120 mEq/L with seizures, coma, or severe neurological symptoms). This is a neurological emergency.
- **Goal:** Emergency correction of Na⁺ by 4-6 mEq/L over the first few hours. The correction rate must not exceed 8-12 mEq/L in 24 hours, to avoid osmotic demyelination syndrome (central pontine myelinolysis).
- **Dosage:** 100-150 mL IV bolus of 3% NaCl over 10-20 minutes. Serum Na⁺ must be checked every 1-2 hours.

### 7.3 Demeclocycline

**Mechanism of Action:** A tetracycline antibiotic that induces a reversible, pharmacological form of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. It inhibits ADH binding to the V2 receptor and disrupts the intracellular cAMP generation in the collecting duct, impairing water reabsorption.

- **Indication:** Historical treatment for chronic SIADH when fluid restriction fails. Rarely used today due to the availability of tolvaptan and its significant side effects (nephrotoxicity, photosensitivity, azotemia).

## PART VIII: DRUGS FOR ANEMIA OF CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE

### 8.1 Erythropoiesis-Stimulating Agents (ESAs)

**Drug Names:** Epoetin alfa (Epogen, Procrit), Darbepoetin alfa (Aranesp), Methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta (Mircera)

**Mechanism of Action:** Recombinant forms of endogenous erythropoietin. They bind to and activate the EPO receptor on erythroid progenitor cells (CFU-E) in the bone marrow, activating the JAK2/STAT5 signaling pathway. This inhibits apoptosis of these cells and promotes their proliferation and differentiation into mature red blood cells.²⁸

- **Key Guideline (KDIGO 2024 Anemia in CKD):** Initiate ESA when hemoglobin (Hb) falls below 10.0 g/dL. The goal is to reduce the need for red blood cell transfusions, not to normalize Hb. The target Hb is 10.0-11.5 g/dL. Higher targets (Hb >12-13 g/dL) have been consistently associated with increased risk of cardiovascular events (stroke, MI, hypertension) and vascular access thrombosis.²⁹
- **Contraindications:** Uncontrolled hypertension, pure red cell aplasia (PRCA) from previous ESA, known hypersensitivity.
- **Dosage:** Individualized to patient weight, Hb level, and rate of response. Subcutaneous or IV (IV preferred in hemodialysis). Epoetin alfa: 50-100 units/kg SC/IV three times weekly. Darbepoetin alfa: 0.45 mcg/kg SC/IV once weekly. Mircera: 0.6 mcg/kg SC/IV every two weeks.

### Important Side Effects:

  - *Hypertension:* A direct effect of raising hematocrit, increasing blood viscosity, and vascular resistance.
  - *Thrombosis of Vascular Access:* Increased thrombogenicity.
  - *Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):* A rare, devastating, antibody-mediated reaction where the patient's immune system produces neutralizing anti-EPO antibodies that cross-react with endogenous EPO, causing a sudden, severe, transfusion-dependent anemia. Most strongly associated with the epoetin alfa formulation Eprex when administered subcutaneously.

### 8.2 Hypoxia-Inducible Factor Prolyl Hydroxylase Inhibitors (HIF-PHIs)

**Drug Names:** Roxadustat (Evrenzo), Vadadustat, Daprodustat (Jesduvroq)

**Mechanism of Action:** These are oral, small-molecule agents that inhibit prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) enzymes. PHD enzymes normally hydroxylate the alpha subunit of HIF (HIF-α), tagging it for von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) protein-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. By inhibiting PHD, the drug stabilizes HIF-α, allowing it to dimerize with HIF-β, translocate to the nucleus, and activate the transcription of multiple hypoxia-responsive genes. This results in a coordinated, physiological response: increased production of endogenous EPO, increased expression of transferrin receptors and iron transporters (DMT1, ferroportin), and reduced hepcidin levels, thereby improving iron mobilization and utilization.³⁰

- **Advantage over ESAs:** Oral administration; may be effective in ESA-hyporesponsive patients due to inflammation (as they lower hepcidin); achieve a more stable Hb response; do not cause supraphysiologic plasma EPO levels.
- **Contraindications:** Pregnancy, breastfeeding.
- **Important Side Effects:** Thromboembolic events (PE, DVT, vascular access thrombosis), hyperkalemia, and concerns about promoting tumor growth and progression (via HIF target genes like VEGF), which has limited broader use.

## PART IX: DRUGS FOR CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE-MINERAL BONE DISORDER (CKD-MBD)

### 9.1 Phosphate Binders

These are taken with meals to bind dietary phosphate in the GI tract, forming insoluble, non-absorbable complexes that are excreted in feces, thereby preventing hyperphosphatemia.³¹

### Calcium-Based Binders:

- *Calcium Carbonate (Tums, Os-Cal):* Cheapest, widely available. Each 500 mg tablet provides 200 mg elemental calcium. Risk of hypercalcemia and positive calcium balance, which can promote vascular calcification. Must be taken with food to act as a binder; between meals, it acts as a calcium supplement.
- *Calcium Acetate (PhosLo, Phoslyra):* Binds more phosphate per mg of elemental calcium absorbed than carbonate, thus has a lower calcium load and lower risk of hypercalcemia. It is the preferred calcium-based binder in the KDIGO guidelines for patients with a need.

### Non-Calcium-Based Binders:

- *Sevelamer Carbonate (Renvela):* A non-absorbed, calcium-free, metal-free, polymeric amine. It binds phosphate via ion exchange and also acts as a bile acid sequestrant, which lowers LDL cholesterol. Does not cause hypercalcemia. Expensive. Side effects: GI bloating, constipation.
- *Lanthanum Carbonate (Fosrenol):* A chewable, non-calcium binder. Very effective phosphate-binding capacity. Theoretically, there was a concern for tissue accumulation of lanthanum, a rare earth metal, but long-term studies have not shown significant toxicity.
- *Sucroferric Oxyhydroxide (Velphoro):* A chewable, iron-based, calcium-free phosphate binder with a high phosphate-binding capacity. It causes a characteristic dark discoloration of the stool. Provides some iron supplementation.
- *Ferric Citrate (Auryxia):* An iron-based phosphate binder that also acts as an oral iron supplement, treating both hyperphosphatemia and iron deficiency anemia in CKD. The citrate component can increase aluminum absorption, so its use is contraindicated with aluminum-containing medications.

### 9.2 Vitamin D Receptor Agonists (VDRAs)

**Indication:** Treatment of secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) in CKD.

**Drug Names:**

- *Calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol):* The natural, active hormone. Directly suppresses PTH gene transcription and synthesis in the parathyroid gland. High risk of inducing hypercalcemia and hyperphosphatemia due to increased intestinal absorption. IV formulation for hemodialysis patients.
- *Paricalcitol (Zemplar):* A synthetic vitamin D analog (19-nor-1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D2). It more potently suppresses PTH with a lesser calcemic and phosphatemic effect than calcitriol.
- *Doxercalciferol (Hectorol):* A prohormone (1-alpha-hydroxyvitamin D2), which is activated by hepatic 25-hydroxylation to the active 1,25-dihydroxy D2.

### 9.3 Calcimimetics

**Drug Name:** Cinacalcet (Sensipar)

**Mechanism of Action:** An allosteric activator (positive allosteric modulator) of the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) on the chief cells of the parathyroid gland. It increases the sensitivity of the CaSR to extracellular calcium. This "tricks" the parathyroid gland into sensing a higher calcium level than is actually present, thereby reducing PTH synthesis and secretion. It also lowers serum calcium and phosphate.³²

- **Indication:** Secondary hyperparathyroidism in dialysis patients. Parathyroid carcinoma and primary hyperparathyroidism in patients unable to undergo parathyroidectomy.
- **Dosage:** 30 mg PO once daily, titrated up to 180 mg/day. Serum calcium and PTH must be monitored closely. Hypocalcemia is the dose-limiting toxicity.
- **Side Effects:** Nausea/vomiting (common), hypocalcemia.

**Drug Name:** Etelcalcetide (Parsabiv)

**Mechanism of Action:** A synthetic peptide calcimimetic, given intravenously three times a week at the end of hemodialysis. Same mechanism as cinacalcet, but via a different binding site on the CaSR.

- **Advantage:** Avoids oral non-adherence, a major issue in long-term dialysis care. More potent in lowering PTH.

**PART X: IMMUNOSUPPRESSIVE AND DISEASE-MODIFYING AGENTS FOR GLOMERULAR DISEASESThis section covers the complex pharmacotherapy of immune-mediated glomerulonephritides. All these agents require specialist administration.**

### 10.1 Glucocorticoids (Prednisone, Methylprednisolone)

- **Mechanism:** Bind to the cytoplasmic glucocorticoid receptor, translocate to the nucleus, and modulate transcription of inflammatory genes. Broad immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory effects: inhibit NF-κB, suppress cytokine production (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α), reduce leukocyte trafficking, and induce apoptosis of lymphocytes.
- **Indications:** First-line therapy for minimal change disease, primary FSGS, lupus nephritis (class III/IV), ANCA-associated vasculitis, and anti-GBM disease.

### 10.2 Calcineurin Inhibitors (Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus)

- **Mechanism:** Bind to intracellular immunophilin proteins (cyclophilin for cyclosporine, FKBP12 for tacrolimus). The drug-immunophilin complex inhibits the phosphatase activity of calcineurin, preventing the dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation of Nuclear Factor of Activated T-cells (NFAT). This blocks T-cell IL-2 transcription and activation.
- **Indications:** Steroid-dependent or steroid-resistant minimal change disease, FSGS, and as maintenance therapy for proliferative lupus nephritis.
- **Renal Toxicity:** Both drugs are powerfully nephrotoxic. They cause acute, reversible, dose-dependent afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction (AKI), and chronic, irreversible tubulointerstitial fibrosis (striped fibrosis). Their use requires therapeutic drug monitoring (trough levels).

### 10.3 Antimetabolites (Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF), Mycophenolic Acid)

- **Mechanism:** MMF is a prodrug of mycophenolic acid. Mycophenolic acid is a non-competitive, selective, and reversible inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH), the rate-limiting enzyme in the *de novo* synthesis of guanosine nucleotides. B and T lymphocytes lack the salvage pathway for purine synthesis and are exquisitely dependent on the *de novo* pathway, making them targeted by this drug.
- **Indications:** First-line maintenance therapy (alongside glucocorticoids) for lupus nephritis (class III/IV/V), showing superiority to cyclophosphamide in the ALMS trial. It is also used in ANCA-associated vasculitis maintenance and other proliferative GN. It is a teratogen (FDA Pregnancy Category D) and is contraindicated in pregnancy.

### 10.4 Alkylating Agents (Cyclophosphamide)

- **Mechanism:** A prodrug activated by hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes to phosphoramide mustard. This alkylates DNA, forming cross-links and causing strand breakage and apoptosis of rapidly proliferating cells, including B and T lymphocytes. It is a powerful immunosuppressant but carries severe long-term toxicity.
- **Indications:** Severe, organ-threatening lupus nephritis, ANCA-associated vasculitis, and anti-GBM disease (with plasmapheresis). It is a cornerstone of induction therapy.
- **Critical Toxicities:** Hemorrhagic cystitis (due to the metabolite acrolein, prevented by aggressive hydration and mesna), cytopenias, gonadal failure, teratogenicity, and a significantly increased lifetime risk of bladder cancer and hematologic malignancies. It is a temporizing bridge to a safer maintenance agent (e.g., azathioprine or rituximab).

### 10.5 Anti-CD20 Monoclonal Antibodies (Rituximab, Ofatumumab)

- **Mechanism:** Chimeric (rituximab) or fully humanized (ofatumumab) monoclonal antibody directed against the CD20 antigen, a cell-surface phosphoprotein expressed on pre-B cells and mature B lymphocytes. Binding causes B-cell lysis through complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), and direct induction of apoptosis. This results in a profound, sustained depletion of circulating B cells, including the precursors of antibody-producing plasma cells.³³
- **Indications:** A first-line induction agent (with glucocorticoids) for ANCA-associated vasculitis (RAVE trial), membranous nephropathy (especially in PLA2R-antibody positive disease), steroid-dependent minimal change disease, and lupus nephritis. Avoids the long-term risks of cyclophosphamide.

### 10.6 Complement Inhibitors

- **Eculizumab (Soliris) and Ravulizumab (Ultomiris):** Monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the terminal complement protein C5, blocking its cleavage to C5a and C5b, and preventing the formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC, C5b-9). They are the treatment of choice for atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS) and C3 glomerulopathy with documented overactivity of the alternative complement pathway.
- **Avacopan (Tavneos):** A novel, oral, small-molecule antagonist of the C5a receptor (C5aR1). It blocks the pro-inflammatory effects of C5a (neutrophil chemotaxis, activation). It was approved as an adjunctive therapy to glucocorticoids for ANCA-associated vasculitis, allowing for a significant reduction in glucocorticoid exposure.

### 10.7 Endothelin Receptor Antagonists

- **Sparsentan (Filspari):** A dual-acting, small-molecule antagonist of both the endothelin type A receptor (ETAR) and the angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R). By blocking ETAR, it reduces endothelin-1-mediated vasoconstriction, podocyte injury, and fibrosis. It received accelerated FDA approval for the reduction of proteinuria in adults with primary IgA nephropathy at high risk of disease progression.³⁴

## PART XI: DRUGS FOR URINARY TRACT INFECTIONS

Choice is guided by local antibiograms, patient allergy profile, renal function, and infection severity.

### 11.1 Nitrofurantoin (Macrobid, Macrodantin)

- **Mechanism:** After renal excretion and concentration in the urine, bacterial nitroreductases reduce nitrofurantoin to highly reactive, toxic intermediates that attack ribosomal proteins, DNA, and the bacterial cell wall. It is active against most common *E. coli* and *Enterococcus faecalis*.
- **Contraindication:** Contraindicated if eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² because insufficient drug is secreted into the urine for efficacy, and the systemic accumulation of toxic metabolites can cause irreversible peripheral neuropathy.

### 11.2 Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX, Bactrim)

- **Mechanism:** Sequential blockade of two steps in bacterial folate synthesis: sulfamethoxazole inhibits dihydropteroate synthase, and trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase.
- **Renal Side Effects:** Can cause a reversible, non-pathological rise in serum creatinine by inhibiting tubular secretion of creatinine. It is a notorious cause of drug-induced hyperkalemia, especially in the elderly and those with renal impairment, by blocking the apical ENaC in the collecting duct, similar to amiloride. It can cause crystalluria; adequate hydration is required.

### 11.3 Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin)

- **Mechanism:** Inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV.
- **Renal Dosing:** Dose adjustment is required for levofloxacin in renal impairment. Ciprofloxacin requires less adjustment. They are first-line agents for acute pyelonephritis and complicated UTIs. Growing *E. coli* resistance is a major public health concern.

### 11.4 Fosfomycin Trometamol (Monurol)

- **Mechanism:** Inhibits the first step of bacterial cell wall synthesis (MurA, enolpyruvyl transferase).
- **Indication:** Uncomplicated cystitis in women. It is a single-dose (3 g) oral powder that is dissolved in water. It is a critical antibiotic for multi-drug resistant (MDR) gram-negative UTIs, as it retains activity against many ESBL-producing organisms, though resistance is increasing.

## PART XII: DRUGS FOR NEPHROLITHIASIS

### 12.1 Thiazide Diuretics (Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone, Indapamide)

- **Indication:** Prevention of recurrent calcium oxalate and calcium phosphate stones in patients with idiopathic hypercalciuria. The drug reduces urinary calcium excretion (see Section 1.4). This is the standard of care.³⁵

### 12.2 Alkali Therapy (Potassium Citrate)

- **Indication:** Treatment and prevention of uric acid stones, cystine stones, and calcium oxalate stones in patients with hypocitraturia. Citrate is an inhibitor of stone formation; it complexes with calcium in the urine, reducing the availability of calcium to bind to oxalate or phosphate. The alkali load also increases urine pH, which is critical because uric acid and cystine are poorly soluble in acidic urine.

### 12.3 Allopurinol and Febuxostat (Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors)

- **Indication:** Prevention of recurrent uric acid stones and calcium oxalate stones in patients with hyperuricosuria. They reduce uric acid production.

## PART XIII: DRUGS FOR POLYCYSTIC KIDNEY DISEASE

**Tolvaptan (Jinarc)** is the only FDA-approved drug shown to slow the increase in total kidney volume and the decline in renal function in adults with rapidly progressive ADPKD (see Section 1.7). Meticulous monitoring for hepatotoxicity is mandatory.

## PART XIV: ANTIHYPERTENSIVE AGENTS WITH SPECIFIC RENAL INDICATIONS

- **Labetalol:** IV bolus is a first-line agent for hypertensive emergencies in renal patients, as it does not rely on renal excretion and does not alter cerebral blood flow.
- **Clevidipine and Nicardipine:** IV calcium channel blockers, ideal for precise titration in hypertensive emergencies with AKI.

## PART XV: ANTICOAGULATION IN RENAL IMPAIRMENT AND NEPHROTIC SYNDROME

Patients with nephrotic syndrome (especially membranous nephropathy with serum albumin <2.0 g/dL) have a hypercoagulable state due to renal loss of antithrombin III and protein C and S. Patients with atrial fibrillation and CKD have a high stroke and bleeding risk.

- **Warfarin (Coumadin):** Vitamin K antagonist. Historically the standard. In CKD, it accelerates vascular calcification (by inhibiting Matrix Gla Protein, a potent vascular calcification inhibitor), causing "calcific uremic arteriolopathy" (calciphylaxis), a dreaded and frequently fatal complication. This risk is unique to warfarin among anticoagulants.
- **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs):** Apixaban (Eliquis), Rivaroxaban (Xarelto), Edoxaban, Dabigatran (Pradaxa). The **2025 KDIGO** and cardiology guidelines generally favor DOACs (specifically apixaban, due to its least renal-dependent clearance) over warfarin in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation and CKD (eGFR 15-59), as they offer a better safety profile (less intracranial hemorrhage) and do not cause calciphylaxis. Dose adjustment for eGFR is mandatory. They are contraindicated in severe CKD (dabigatran if eGFR <30; rivaroxaban and edoxaban if eGFR <15; apixaban can be used down to eGFR 15 with dose reduction).³⁶

## PART XVI: MISCELLANEOUS RENOPROTECTIVE AND SUPPORTIVE AGENTS

- **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC):** Free radical scavenger; historically used for contrast-induced nephropathy prophylaxis. Its efficacy is now highly controversial, with most large RCTs showing no benefit. It is no longer universally recommended.
- **Sodium Bicarbonate Infusion:** For contrast nephropathy prophylaxis, the theory is that alkalinizing the tubular fluid inhibits hydroxyl radical generation. Evidence is mixed but it is used based on the PRESERVE trial showing no benefit over saline, but still used in some centers for high-risk patients.
- **Desmopressin (DDAVP):** A synthetic V2 agonist. It is used not for renal indication, but for the reversal of uremic platelet dysfunction (by releasing von Willebrand factor and Factor VIII from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies) in a bleeding patient with advanced CKD.
